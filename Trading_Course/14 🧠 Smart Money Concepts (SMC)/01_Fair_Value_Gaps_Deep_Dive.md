## 1. The Core Philosophy: What is an FVG in algorithmic terms?

When retail traders look at a chart, they see support and resistance lines, trendlines, and candlestick patterns. **Institutions do not see patterns; they see liquidity and imbalances.** 

A **Fair Value Gap (FVG)**, also known as an Imbalance or Inefficiency, is a specific price range where a massive amount of liquidity was injected into the market in a single direction (either aggressive buying or aggressive selling). Because this price movement happened so rapidly, the market did not get a chance to offer the counter-party (the buyers during a sell-off, or the sellers during a rally) a "fair" chance to trade.

In simple terms: Price "jumped" over a specific level due to immense volume, leaving a void. 

### Why do FVGs matter?
The market algorithm is designed to achieve **equilibrium**. An FVG represents disequilibrium. Think of it like a vacuum in space—the market structurally *must* return to that void eventually to offer fair value to both buyers and sellers. We use these vacuums as our high-probability entry targets.

## 2. The Anatomy of an FVG

An FVG is strictly a **3-candle formation**. It does not exist with 2 candles, and looking beyond 3 candles will confuse you. 

### Bullish FVG (BISI - Buyside Imbalance Sellside Inefficiency)
A Bullish FVG occurs during an explosive upward move.
- **Candle 1:** A bullish candle (starts the move). We care about its **High**.
- **Candle 2:** A massive institutional bullish displacement candle. This is the candle that breaks structure and creates the gap.
- **Candle 3:** A bullish or bearish candle. We care about its **Low**.

**The Gap:** If the Low of Candle 3 does NOT touch or overlap with the High of Candle 1, the empty space between them on Candle 2's body is your Bullish FVG.

### Bearish FVG (SIBI - Sellside Imbalance Buyside Inefficiency)
A Bearish FVG occurs during an explosive downward move.
- **Candle 1:** A bearish candle. We care about its **Low**.
- **Candle 2:** A massive institutional bearish displacement candle.
- **Candle 3:** A bullish or bearish candle. We care about its **High**.

**The Gap:** If the High of Candle 3 does NOT touch or overlap with the Low of Candle 1, the empty space between them is your Bearish FVG.

## 3. The Psychology Behind The Gap

### Why does the market return to the FVG? 
Imagine a central bank needs to buy 10,000 lots of EUR/USD. They can't just click "buy" because the market doesn't have 10,000 lots of sellers available at the current price. If they buy all at once, they will cause a massive price spike (generating Candle 2). 

Because they caused this spike, many of their orders were triggered at unfavorable, higher prices. The algorithm then ceases aggressive buying, allowing retail traders to naturally push the price back down. Once the price returns to the "gap" where the original buying started, the institution activates the rest of their pending orders to accumulate more positions at a wholesale (discount) price. 

**This is why an FVG acts as a magnet for price, and then a trampoline.**
